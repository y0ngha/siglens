## How it looks

Price rises, slides down gradually, then climbs back to form a U-shaped base (the cup). Once the right rim recovers to near the left rim, a small dip (the handle) forms. The name comes from its resemblance to a cup with a handle.

## What it tells you

The cup is read as selling pressure being absorbed slowly while buying builds. The handle is seen as a last round of selling by holders who could not stand the shakeout. When price climbs above the handle, the advance is expected to resume.

Textbooks treat the pattern as complete when the close finishes above the handle's upper line. Siglens uses the right rim's price as the breakout level instead of the handle's upper line. Volume at least 50% above average on the breakout gives the signal more weight. The shape counts as better when the cup depth is 12% to 33% of the prior advance and the handle dips less than 10% from the rim.

In the tabulation of Thomas Bulkowski, who counted what actually happened after patterns across decades of US stock charts and published the results in his books, the cup and handle came 3rd of 39 bullish patterns in the performance ranking, which orders patterns by how far price went after the breakout. However, when he examined 300 cups from 1990 to 2024, 47% fell sharply within two months of the breakout. A target is the price reached if the move extends by the pattern's height (the cup depth): a reference value based on how often past patterns went that far, not a promise. The invalidation level is the price at which the pattern is considered broken.

## How Siglens detects it

Siglens takes two swing highs among clearly turning highs and lows (swings: turning points where price reversed by more than 1.5 times the [ATR](/guide/indicators/atr), the average range of recent bars) as the two rims of the cup. When the space between them dips in a gentle U and a shallow pullback (the handle) forms after the right rim, it treats the shape as a cup and handle.

- The two rims must be at least 30 bars apart.
- The price gap between the rims must be within 7% of the higher rim's price and no more than 25% of the cup depth. Because the right rim often recovers short of the left, this is looser than the textbook 5%.
- No bar between the rims may rise more than 0.25 times ATR above the lower rim.
- The cup depth (from the lower rim to the bottom) must be at least 2.5 times ATR and at least a set share of price (0.5% on 5- to 30-minute bars, 1% on 1- to 4-hour bars, 3% on daily bars).
- U shape: at least 40% of the closes between the rims must sit in the lower third of the cup's depth. Sharp V shapes are excluded.
- The left rim must be higher than every bar before it over a stretch half the cup's width. That means price climbed into it.
- The bottom must fall in the middle half of the cup in time.
- The right rim must be 5 to 20 bars before the last bar.
- The handle low is the lowest swing low after the right rim. If it drops below the cup's midpoint (a dip of more than half the cup depth), Siglens does not treat the shape as a cup and handle.

The breakout level is the right rim's price. The measured target adds the cup depth (from the right rim to the bottom) to it, and the conservative target adds half. The invalidation level is the handle low.

## Watch out for

- A V-shaped bottom does not count as a cup.
- If the cup's depth is more than 40% of the prior advance, it is considered less reliable.
- If the handle drops into the lower half of the cup, the pattern is invalid.
- If volume does not rise on the breakout, the signal is weak.
- A handle lasting more than 4 weeks may mean the breakout attempt failed.
- A close below the handle low means the pattern has broken.
