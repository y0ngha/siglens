## How it looks

Price rises, slides down gradually, then climbs back to form a U-shaped base (the cup). Once the right rim recovers to near the left rim, a small dip (the handle) forms. The name comes from its resemblance to a cup with a handle.

## What it tells you

The cup is read as selling pressure being absorbed slowly while buying builds. The handle is seen as a last round of selling by holders who could not stand the shakeout. When price clears the handle's upper line, the advance is expected to resume.

The pattern completes when the close finishes above the handle's upper line. It is more convincing if volume is at least 50% above average at that point. It is also viewed more favorably when the cup depth is 12% to 33% of the prior advance and the handle dip is under 10%. In Thomas Bulkowski's tabulation, the cup and handle ranked 3rd among 39 patterns. However, he examined 300 cups from 1990 to 2024 and found that 47% fell sharply within two months of the breakout. That is why the target should not be treated as a price the stock will surely reach.

## How Siglens detects it

Siglens confirms a swing high or swing low once price has reversed by at least 1.5 times the [ATR](/guide/indicators/atr) (the average range of one bar), and uses two swing highs as the two rims of the cup. All of the following conditions must be met.

- The two rims must be at least 30 bars apart.
- The price difference between the rims must be within 7% of the higher rim and at most 25% of the cup's depth. This is looser than the textbook 5%, because the right side often fails to recover as far as the left.
- No bar inside the cup may rise above the lower rim by more than 0.25 times ATR. The legs coming down from and back up to the rims are excluded from this check.
- The cup's depth (from the lower rim to the bottom) must be at least 2.5 times ATR and at least a set share of price (0.5% on 5- to 30-minute bars, 1% on 1- to 4-hour bars, 3% on daily bars).
- U-shape condition: at least 40% of the closes between the two rims must lie in the lower third of the cup's depth.
- The left rim must be higher than every bar in the stretch before it (half the cup's width in length), meaning it is a high reached by rising. The bottom must fall within the middle half of the cup in time.
- The right rim must be 5 to 20 bars before the last bar.
- The handle low is the lowest swing low after the right rim. It must not fall below the cup's midpoint in height.

The breakout level is the right rim's price. The measured target is that price plus the cup's depth (from the right rim to the bottom), and the conservative target is that price plus half the depth. The invalidation level is the handle low. On the chart, the pattern is drawn continuously from the left rim to the handle low.

## Watch out for

- A V-shaped bottom does not count as a cup.
- If the cup's depth is more than 40% of the prior advance, it is considered less reliable.
- If the handle drops into the lower half of the cup, the pattern is invalid.
- If volume does not rise on the breakout, it is considered less reliable.
- A handle lasting more than 4 weeks may mean the breakout attempt failed.
- A close below the handle low means the pattern has broken.
