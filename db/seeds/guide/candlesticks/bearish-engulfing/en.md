## How it looks

It has two bars. The first is bullish. The second is bearish: it opens at or above the first bar's close and closes at or below the first bar's open. The second body fully wraps the first.

## What it tells you

One bar erased the prior advance, so it is read as a clue that control passed from buyers to sellers. Textbooks give it weight when it appears at the end of an uptrend.

## How SIGLENS detects it

SIGLENS compares the two bodies only. It ignores the shadows and does not check whether an advance came first, so look at the chart yourself for the prior trend.

- The first bar is bullish and the second is bearish.
- The second bar opens at or above the first bar's close.
- The second bar closes at or below the first bar's open.

Thomas Bulkowski, who counted what price actually did after each pattern across decades of US stock charts and published the results in books, found a reversal rate (how often price turned down as expected) of 79%, the 5th highest of 103 candlesticks. When a later close fell below the pattern's low (a downward breakout), the average 10-day decline was 3.56% in a bull market and 5.92% in a bear market.

Its performance rank (a ranking by how far price went afterward) is low, though: 91st of 103. It gets the direction right often; the low rank comes from the times it was wrong. Downward breakouts did fine (25th in bull markets, 21st in bear markets), but when price broke upward against the pattern, it barely rose afterward and ranked at the very bottom of all candlesticks (103rd and 100th). Those failures drag the overall rank down.

It carries more weight when:

- It follows a clear uptrend and sits near resistance
- The second body is noticeably larger than recent bars
- The second bar's volume is above normal
- [RSI](/guide/indicators/rsi) is overbought (price has risen so far, so fast that a pullback is considered possible) or price has touched the upper [Bollinger Band](/guide/indicators/bollinger-bands)

## Watch out for

Until a close below the pattern's low appears, this is only a reversal clue. If a close instead rises above the pattern's high, drop the bearish read, but don't expect a strong rally out of that failure either.

A bearish engulfing in a sideways market (where [ADX](/guide/indicators/adx), which measures trend strength, is below 20 and there is no clear direction) is hard to trust. In markets that trade around the clock (crypto), the open sits right on the prior close, so this shape forms easily. Apply stricter confirmation there.
