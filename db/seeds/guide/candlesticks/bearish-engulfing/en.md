## How it looks

It has two bars. The first is bullish. The second is bearish: it opens at or above the first bar's close and closes at or below the first bar's open. The second body fully wraps the first.

## What it tells you

One bar erased the prior advance, so it is read as a clue that control passed from buyers to sellers. Textbooks give it weight when it appears at the end of an uptrend.

## How Siglens detects it

Siglens compares bodies only. If the second bearish bar opens at or above the first bullish bar's close and closes at or below its open, it is a bearish engulfing. Shadows are ignored. Siglens does not check the preceding trend, so check the chart yourself for a prior advance.

In Thomas Bulkowski's data the bearish reversal rate was 79%, ranking 5th of 103 candlesticks by reversal rate. When a later close fell below the pattern's low (a downside breakout), the average 10-day decline was 3.56% in a bull market and 5.92% in a bear market.

Its overall performance rank is low, though: 91st of 103. The reason is the failures. When price broke upward instead, against the pattern, performance was among the worst of all candlesticks.

It carries more weight when:

- It follows a clear uptrend and sits near resistance
- The second body is noticeably larger than recent bars
- The second bar's volume is above normal
- [RSI](/guide/indicators/rsi) is overbought or price has touched the upper [Bollinger Band](/guide/indicators/bollinger-bands)

## Watch out for

Until a close below the pattern's low appears, this is only a reversal clue. If a close instead rises above the pattern's high, drop the bearish read, but don't expect a strong rally out of that failure either.

A bearish engulfing in a sideways market ([ADX](/guide/indicators/adx) below 20) isn't reliable. In markets that trade around the clock (crypto), the open sits right on the prior close, so this shape forms easily. Apply stricter confirmation there.
