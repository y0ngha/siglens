## How it looks

- Above the stomach: a long bearish bar followed by a bullish bar that opens and closes at or above the midpoint of the first body. Textbooks read it as a bullish reversal within a downtrend.
- Below the stomach: a long bullish bar followed by a bearish bar that opens and closes at or below the midpoint of the first body. Textbooks read it as a bearish reversal within an uptrend.

## What it tells you

The second bar traded from the start at a level that had already recovered half of the first body. It is notable because it goes against the first bar's move from the open.

These are the counts of Thomas Bulkowski, who tallied what actually happened after patterns across decades of US stock charts and published the results in books. The reversal rate is the share that actually turned in the expected direction, and the performance rank orders 103 candle patterns by how far price went afterward.

- Above the stomach: 66% bullish reversal, performance rank 31st. In another of his studies it ranked 1st among common bullish reversal candles, with an average gain of 2.74% ten days later.
- Below the stomach: 60% bearish reversal, performance rank 59th, in the middle.

The bullish version clearly did better than the bearish one. Above the stomach worked best where a pullback inside an uptrend ended, or near the yearly low (the lower third of the year's price range). Below the stomach worked best at a brief bounce inside a downtrend.

These figures were measured on patterns that came after a trend (above the stomach after a decline, below the stomach after an advance). The same shape with no trend before it is just two bars wobbling in a sideways market.

## How Siglens detects it

Siglens looks for a long bar followed by an opposite-color bar that starts beyond the halfway point of the first body.

- The first body is at least 60% of its own high-to-low range.
- The second bar is the opposite color.
- The second bar opens beyond the midpoint of the first body: at or above it for above the stomach, at or below it for below the stomach.
- The preceding trend is not checked.

Because Siglens does not check the trend, the figures above cannot be carried over as-is to what Siglens detects. Check on the chart what trend came before.

## Watch out for

Above the stomach is confirmed only when a later close moves above the higher of the two bars' highs; below the stomach, when a later close moves below the lower of the two lows. Until then it is only a clue. It matters more near support (above) or resistance (below) with high volume on the second bar. A pattern that appears when [ADX](/guide/indicators/adx), which measures trend strength, is below 20 and the market has no clear direction is weak, and below the stomach is on the weak side even on its own.
