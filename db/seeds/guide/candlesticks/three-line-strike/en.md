## How it looks

It has four bars. Three same-color bars close progressively in one direction, then the fourth bar wipes out that move in one go. The fourth bar opens beyond the third close and closes beyond the first bar's open.

- Bullish three-line strike: three bullish bars (rising closes), then a bearish bar that opens at or above the third close and closes below the first open.
- Bearish three-line strike: three bearish bars (falling closes), then a bullish bar that opens at or below the third close and closes above the first open.

## The name and the actual direction differ

The traditional name describes a continuation pattern, where a bullish three-line strike means the advance continues and a bearish one means the decline continues. But in Bulkowski's data both often reversed in the direction of the fourth bar.

## How Siglens detects it

Siglens checks only the shape of the four bars, not the preceding trend. For the bullish three-line strike, the closes of the three bullish bars must rise in turn, and the fourth bar must open at or above the third close and close below the first bar's open. The bearish version is the opposite. The body lengths of the first three bars are not considered.

Direction follows the data, not the name. A bullish three-line strike is read as bearish and a bearish one as bullish. In effect, the fourth bar is treated as the signal.

Thomas Bulkowski's data shows:

- Bullish three-line strike: 65% bearish reversal. Ranked 2nd of 103 candlesticks for overall performance.
- Bearish three-line strike: 84% bullish reversal. Ranked 1st for overall performance, the best of 103 candlesticks.

Both are rare, so the statistics rest on few cases. The bearish three-line strike did better where a pullback inside an uptrend ended or near the yearly low (the lower third of the year's price range), and the bullish one did better with large bars in a bear market. It matters more when the fourth bar is large and appears at support or resistance.

## Watch out for

It is weak in a sideways market ([ADX](/guide/indicators/adx) below 20) or when the bars are very small. A close beyond the fourth bar's far end confirms the breakout. The figures are just rates from the past, so don't assume it will play out that way this time.
