## How it looks

Three big-bodied bars in the same direction appear in a row. Each bar opens inside the previous bar's body and closes beyond the previous close.

- Three white soldiers: three bullish bars, each closing higher. Textbooks read it as a bullish reversal after a decline.
- Three black crows: three bearish bars, each closing lower. Textbooks read it as a bearish reversal after an advance.

## What it tells you

One side won three times in a row, so it is read as a clue that the earlier trend has ended and strength is building in the opposite direction.

## How Siglens detects it

All three bars must be the same color, with bodies at least 60% of their high-to-low range. The second and third bars must open inside the previous bar's body. For white soldiers, each open must be above the previous open and below the previous close. The preceding trend is not checked.

In Thomas Bulkowski's data the bullish reversal rate for three white soldiers was 82%, 3rd of 103 in the reversal-rate ranking, and the bearish reversal rate for three black crows was 78%, both high. But three white soldiers didn't travel far after the breakout, so their overall performance ranked only 32nd of 103, low compared with the reversal rate. Three black crows ranked 3rd for overall performance.

The high reversal rate has a structural reason too. The pattern already ends near the high (near the low for black crows), so it easily gets judged as having broken out in the same direction.

It carries more weight when:

- A clear trend in the opposite direction came before
- The bodies are large and the wicks are short
- Volume grows across the three bars
- It breaks through the 20-day [EMA](/guide/indicators/ema) or a key price level
- For black crows, the bars are large (Bulkowski's data showed better performance)

## Watch out for

If it appears after a big move in the same direction, it can signal exhaustion. If the third bar is unusually long, treat it as overheated and be wary of chasing. Bulkowski said three white soldiers appearing as a pullback inside a downtrend often saw the decline continue. Near a major resistance or support, keep a failed breakout in mind.
